What Are Examples of Keywords?

Published 2026-11-20

TL;DR: Keywords are words or phrases shoppers use to search, and in Etsy titles they usually come from the item’s type, material, style, use, or audience. Good examples match the actual listing, not a random keyword list.

Keywords are words or phrases people type when they search for something. For Etsy sellers, the best ones usually sound like the way a shopper would describe the item. A keyword can be one word or a longer phrase. The point is fit. If the wording matches how someone would search for the product, material, style, use, or audience, it is a strong candidate.

For a broader look at shopper language, see What do you mean by keyword research?. If you want help narrowing possible phrases, How to find low competition keywords for listings covers that next step.

What counts as a keyword

A keyword can be a single word, like "necklace," or a phrase, like "sterling silver necklace." It should reflect the search language a buyer would actually type or say. That may describe the product type, a feature, a use case, or buyer intent, depending on the item.

The same idea can appear in different forms. One listing may need a product-type phrase, another a material phrase, and another an occasion or recipient phrase. The best choice is the one that matches the item cleanly, without turning the title into a string of labels.

Examples of keywords for Etsy listings

Here are plain examples that fit common handmade or vintage listing language:

  • product type: "mug", "wall art", "vintage brooch"
  • material: "ceramic", "oak", "linen"
  • style: "boho", "mid century", "rustic"
  • occasion: "wedding gift", "baby shower", "birthday"
  • recipient: "gift for mom", "for him", "nursery decor"
  • use case: "coffee mug", "desk organizer", "everyday tote"

These are not universal winners. A strong keyword for one listing can be weak for another, because the item itself changes what shoppers are likely to search for.

Worked example: turning product details into keywords

Imagine a hypothetical listing for a handmade oak phone stand with a minimalist look, made as a desk accessory and suitable as a gift for a coworker.

Possible keyword examples from that listing could be:

  • "phone stand"
  • "oak phone stand"
  • "wood phone stand"
  • "minimalist desk accessory"
  • "desk organizer gift"
  • "gift for coworker"

Here, "phone stand" names the core item. "Oak phone stand" and "wood phone stand" add material detail. "Minimalist desk accessory" adds style and use. "Gift for coworker" adds recipient intent.

That breakdown matters because keyword examples should come from real product details, not a random phrase list. If the stand is actually oak, calling it "oak" helps. If it is not, that term does not belong in the title.

How these examples relate to Etsy title writing

For Etsy title writing, the title should use the most relevant search terms while still reading like a real product name. The best keyword examples fit the item cleanly and help a shopper recognize it fast. Readability still matters. If the title becomes a chain of phrases, it is probably too far.

Use Etsy’s own help and seller documentation for title length and formatting decisions. That is the place to check the rules that govern the platform. Then choose keyword examples that stay accurate, useful, and easy to scan.

Why a keyword example should match the listing exactly

A keyword only helps if it describes the actual product. Mismatched terms can confuse buyers and make the listing feel off. If the item is cotton, do not label it linen. If it is modern, do not call it vintage because the word sounds nice on its own.

This is why the safest approach is to choose terms that reflect the listing’s true material, style, use, or audience. That keeps the title honest and useful. It also avoids the overstuffed title problem, where every possible search phrase shows up at once and none of them do enough work.
