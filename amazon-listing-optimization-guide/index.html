Amazon listing optimization: the complete 2026 guide

To optimize an Amazon listing, work the five fields Amazon's search and shoppers actually use: the title, the five bullet points, the backend search terms, the images, and (for brand-registered sellers) A+ Content. Put your primary keyword and product identity at the front of the title; use the bullets to answer buyer objections in their words; fill the backend search terms with synonyms you couldn't fit elsewhere (up to 249 bytes, no repeats); and make image one a clean, zoomable hero. Then A/B test with Amazon's free Manage Your Experiments, which Amazon says can lift sales up to 20%.

The five levers, in priority order

1. Title

The title is the strongest single field. Lead with the brand and the primary keyword phrase, then the key attributes (size, count, material, use). Follow Amazon's category limits and the Amazon Seller Central product-title requirements, keyword-stuffed or all-caps titles can be suppressed. Aim to stay within the 200-character ceiling but front-load the essentials into the first ~80 characters that show on mobile.

2. Bullet points

You get five. Do not list specs; answer the questions and objections buyers actually have (durability, fit, compatibility, what's in the box). The language should mirror how reviewers describe the product, because that is what shoppers search and scan for. Mining your own and competitors' reviews for that language is one of the highest-return steps.

3. Backend search terms

These are invisible to shoppers but indexed by Amazon: up to 249 bytes of additional keywords, synonyms, spelling variants, and related terms you could not fit into the title or bullets. Do not repeat words already in the title (wasted space), do not use competitor brand names, and do not add commas (they count as bytes).

4. Images

Image one must be the product on a pure white background, filling the frame, and zoomable. The remaining slots should show scale, features, in-use context, and any comparison chart. Images move conversion more than almost anything else on the page.

5. A+ Content

If you are brand-registered, A+ Content replaces the plain description with modules of images and text. It is where you tell the brand story and pre-empt returns with clear sizing and use guidance.

Then: research and test

Use Amazon Product Opportunity Explorer to find the search terms and unmet demand behind your category (Amazon claims products launched with its insights have "2.5x higher sales potential" in the first three months). After you rewrite, do not guess which version is better, run Amazon Manage Your Experiments, Amazon's free A/B testing tool, which reports optimized content can lift sales "up to 20%." Where a rewrite tool fits

Research surfaces the keywords; the rewrite is separate work. You can do it by hand with the priorities above, use Helium 10's Scribbles as a coverage checker, or use a dedicated rewrite tool. Listingtonic (Amazon-only, $199 one-time) audits an existing listing and returns copy-paste rewrites for the title, bullets, and backend terms, scored against licensed keyword volumes and review-mined language; it does not promise rankings. It is one option, not a requirement, the free path above works, it just takes longer.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most important fields in an Amazon listing?
In priority order: the title (lead with brand and primary keyword), the five bullet points (answer buyer objections in reviewer language), the backend search terms (up to 249 bytes of synonyms), the images (hero on white, then scale and use), and A+ Content for brand-registered sellers.
How long can Amazon backend search terms be?
Up to 249 bytes. Do not repeat words already in your title, do not use competitor brand names, and avoid commas since they consume bytes.
Does optimizing an Amazon listing actually increase sales?
Amazon's own Manage Your Experiments tool reports optimized content can increase sales by up to 20%, and Product Opportunity Explorer claims 2.5x higher first-quarter sales potential for products launched with its insights. Results depend on execution and testing, not on any single tool.
Should I A/B test my Amazon listing?
Yes, if eligible. Amazon's free Manage Your Experiments lets you test titles, images, bullets, and A+ Content and publishes the winner. It removes the guesswork from which rewrite actually sells better.
