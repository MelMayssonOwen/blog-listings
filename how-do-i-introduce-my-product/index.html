How Do I Introduce My Product?

Published 2026-11-09

TL;DR: Introduce a product by naming the item first, then placing the most buyer-relevant detail near the front. Keep the title focused on identity, use the description for context and limits, and move variants or other decision-making details into the listing sections where shoppers can scan them quickly.

Start with the product itself

Name the item plainly first. Buyers should know immediately what they are looking at. Open with the product, then add the detail that matters most after the item is identified.

That also keeps the title, description, and listing fields from trying to do the same job. The opening should tell shoppers, “yes, this is the thing you clicked,” before it gets clever or promotional.

Lead with the buyer-relevant detail

After the item name, put the feature, use case, or differentiator that most affects the purchase decision near the front. Ask what a buyer is probably wondering, then answer that first. If the product is handcrafted, for example, the main material or standout function may matter more than a padded adjective that sounds nice but tells nobody anything useful.

If you want a broader checklist for what a listing should clarify up front, see What Questions Should a Listing Answer Before Buyers Ask?. It helps because this question is really about removing doubt before the buyer has to scroll and squint.

Keep the title and description roles separate

A simple rule of thumb works well here. Use the title for item identity. Use the description for context, variants, care, compatibility, or other supporting details.

That split keeps the intro clean and makes the listing easier to scan. If the title has to do too much, the opening gets muddy fast. If the description starts with a paragraph full of side quests, buyers have to hunt for the actual product.

Worked example: a hypothetical Etsy-style product introduction

Here is a hypothetical opening that does the job without rambling:

“Handmade ceramic coffee mug with speckled glaze, ideal for everyday use.”

That opening names the product first, adds one buyer-relevant differentiator, and stays concise. For Etsy-specific listing writing, official help says the title can be up to 140 characters, so a strong intro does not need to use the whole space at once. Use the title for the item and the key identifying detail, then let the description handle the rest.

If the product has variants or special limits, move those details out of the intro

If the product comes in different options, or has dimensions, care notes, or other decision-making details, put those where shoppers can scan them quickly. The opening line should stay clean. It should not turn into a miniature spreadsheet.

That means the intro identifies the item, then the listing sections below handle the choices and limits. If the product has variants, the variation fields are usually the better home for those choices, which keeps the headline from doing all the heavy lifting by itself. For that angle, see When a Seller Lists a Product With Multiple Variants, What Should Be Done?. It explains why shared product identity and actual variations should not get tangled together in the headline.
